## 1. Board

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Outline | **100 x 80 mm**, 3 mm corner radius (MR-01: at the 100 x 80 limit) |
| Mounting holes | MH1-MH4, 3.2 mm non-plated (M3), 4 mm in from each edge (MR-04) |

| Nets | 89 |
| Layers | 4: Top signal, L2 ground, L3 3V3, Bottom signal (MR-03) |

The two bottom mounting holes sit inside the isolated islands. They are non-plated with no
copper land, so a metal standoff touches FR-4 only; the holes do not bridge the barrier.

## 2. The isolation barrier is geometry first, rule second

The board is three regions:

```
   y = 80  +------------------------------------------------------+
           |                    HOST  (GND, 3V3, 5V, ...)         |
   y = 35.5+======================================================+   4 mm band, no copper
   y = 31.5+----------------------+####+----------------------+   any layer
           |   ISLAND 0 (GND_ISO0)|####|   ISLAND 1 (GND_ISO1)|
           |   DB9 CH0            |####|   DB9 CH1            |   4 mm band, x = 48..52
   y = 0   +----------------------+####+----------------------+
          x=0                    48   52                    100
```

- **Keep-out regions** on the Keep-Out layer (all layers) cover both bands
  (`tools/altium/keepout.pas`). Any copper placed in them is a DRC violation.
- The **router** treats the bands as hard obstacles on every layer (`router.py`, `KEEPOUT`),
  so no track or via can cross them even before DRC sees it.
- The **placer** marks the bands occupied and keeps every island passive inside its island
  (`place.py`, `region_of()`), so a decoupling capacitor for an isolated part cannot land on
  the host side.
- The **pours** are three separate polygons (`tools/polys.py`): the host region and each
  island, and none of them touches a band. L2 is a solid ground plane on the host side only;
  each island carries its own GND_ISOk pour on L2, top and bottom, as ARCH-003 section 8 asked.
- The **netlist check** (`design.py`, `check()`) refuses any net that joins an island to the
  host side or to the other island (IR-01, IR-05).

Only four parts straddle a band, and each does so by its own isolation rating: the two
**ISO1042** (pins 1-4 host side at y = 38, pins 5-8 bus side at y = 29, TI's 9.1 mm creepage
package) and the two **transformers** (primary pads at y = 39, secondary at y = 28, 5 kV RMS
test, 800 V RMS working). Their bodies cross the band; their copper does not.

The 4 mm of IR-03 is the band width. The ISO1042's own creepage is 9.1 mm and the
transformer's 9.2 mm (IEC 61800-5-1 figure in its datasheet), so the parts, not the board,
set the barrier's rating.

## 3. Stack-up (MR-03)

| Layer | Use |
|---|---|
| L1 Top | signals, all components, GND / GND_ISOk pours |
| L2 | **solid GND plane (host)**; GND_ISO0 and GND_ISO1 pours in the islands |
| L3 | 3V3 pour (host); empty in the islands |
| L4 Bottom | signals, GND / GND_ISOk pours |

1.6 mm FR-4, 1 oz copper. No controlled impedance (ARCH-003 section 8): the 64 MHz clock and
the 8 Mbaud UART are not transmission-line problems on a 100 mm board.

## 4. Design rules (D-03)

| Rule | Value | Why |

## 5. Placement

- **Islands at the bottom, DB9s on the bottom edge** (MR-02, same connector and pinout as
  projects 01 and 02, C-04). Each island holds its transceiver's bus side, the ESD diode, the
  termination network and jumper, the rectifiers and the LP2985.
- **Host side above the band.** SN6501 drivers directly above their transformers; the
  TPS54360B input stage along the left edge next to the screw terminal J4; FPGA in the middle
  with the oscillator above its clock pin (128, top edge of the package); flash below the
  package next to the bank-8 configuration pins (46-54, bottom edge); FT2232H on the right
  next to the USB-C; the three rails along the top edge.
- Every decoupling capacitor is assigned to one supply pin and placed first
  (`place.py`, ER-06 approach from project 02): 19 on the ECP5, 7 on the FT2232H.
- `place.py` checks the fixed parts against each other and against every hole before the
  greedy pass; three overlaps were caught and fixed before anything reached Altium.

## 6. Routing

`tools/router.py` (A*, 0.1 mm grid, 4 layers, escape stubs, plane fan-out vias, rip-up passes)
routed the board with the bands as walls. Power first (0.5 mm), then the clock and CAN pairs,
then signals. One net is still reported unrouted (section 7).

**Gate 3 is not passed.** The board is routed end to end apart from one net, but the check is not
clean: most items are clearance (198) and minimum width on the power nets (159). They have not
been analysed or fixed yet, so no claim is made about their cause. No short circuits are reported.
Clearing this list is the next step, and nothing below (outputs) is valid until it is clean.

**Waiver: silk to solder mask.** Disabled, as in project 02; fabrication houses clip silk from
mask openings as standard and no silk here carries information that would be lost.

## 8. 3D models and outputs

Every part carries a body (`tools/altium/bodies.pas`): KiCad `packages3D` STEP models where one
exists for the package (CC-BY-SA 4.0, see `hardware/3D_Models/README.md`); the oscillator uses
the 3225 crystal model (same outline); the transformer, the 12 x 12 inductor and the screw
terminal are extruded boxes of their datasheet outline and height.

Fabrication and assembly outputs are generated from the standard Altium output jobs, as for
project 02, **once the DRC is clean**. They have not been generated for this revision.
